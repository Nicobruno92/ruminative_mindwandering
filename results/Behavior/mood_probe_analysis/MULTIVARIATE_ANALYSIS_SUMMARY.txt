================================================================================
MULTIVARIATE MOOD (EVA) PREDICTORS OF THOUGHT DIMENSIONS
================================================================================

Analysis Date: 2025-11-13
Script: mood_probe_analysis.py

OVERVIEW
--------
This analysis examines how mood state (measured by EVA scale) predicts the 
dimensions of thought-probes during SART tasks. Unlike univariate analyses,
this uses ALL four EVA dimensions simultaneously as predictors in each model.

MODELS FITTED
-------------
For each thought dimension (onoff, valence, time, selfother, confidence):
  thought_dim ~ EVAtense + EVAfeel + EVAmood + EVAhurt + group + (1|subject_id)

Where:
  - EVAtense: "How tense are you?" (0-10)
  - EVAfeel: "How do you feel?" (0-10)
  - EVAmood: "What is your mood?" (0-10)
  - EVAhurt: "How much does it hurt?" (0-10)
  - group: Controls vs Risk of Depression
  - (1|subject_id): Random intercept for each subject

DATA STRUCTURE
--------------
- 853 probe observations with EVA data
- 33 subjects (457 Controls, 396 Risk of Depression)
- EVA timing strategy:
  * Sart3: Used EVA at beginning (block 1, ~2s onset)
  * Sart1, Sart2, Sart4: Used EVA at end (~1100-1200s onset)

================================================================================
RESULTS SUMMARY
================================================================================

1. VALENCE (Emotional Valence of Thoughts)
-------------------------------------------
Significant predictors:
  - EVAmood: β=3.571, p<0.001 ***
  
Interpretation:
  → Better mood STRONGLY predicts more positive thoughts
  → This is the clearest and strongest effect across all models
  → EVAmood is the only significant predictor when controlling for other mood dimensions

2. SELFOTHER (Self vs Other Orientation)
-----------------------------------------
Significant predictors:
  - EVAmood: β=3.473, p<0.001 ***
  - EVAtense: β=2.830, p=0.005 **
  - EVAfeel: β=-2.568, p=0.021 *
  - Group (RoD): β=12.068, p=0.020 *

Interpretation:
  → Complex pattern with opposing effects:
    * Higher mood and tension → More self-focused thoughts
    * Better feeling → Less self-focused thoughts (more other-focused)
  → Risk of Depression group shows significantly higher self-focus
  → This is the only dimension with a significant group effect

3. ONOFF (Mind-Wandering vs On-Task)
-------------------------------------
Significant predictors:
  - EVAmood: β=2.522, p<0.001 ***
  - EVAfeel: β=-1.248, p=0.037 *

Interpretation:
  → Paradoxical effects:
    * Better mood → Higher onoff (MORE mind-wandering)
    * Better feeling → Lower onoff (LESS mind-wandering, more on-task)
  → These opposing effects suggest mood and feeling capture different aspects

4. TIME (Temporal Orientation)
-------------------------------
Significant predictors:
  - EVAfeel: β=-2.626, p=0.002 **
  - EVAmood: β=1.691, p=0.039 *

Interpretation:
  → Mixed effects on temporal orientation
  → Better feeling → Lower time values
  → Better mood → Higher time values

5. CONFIDENCE (Confidence in Probe Responses)
----------------------------------------------
Significant predictors:
  - EVAhurt: β=1.801, p=0.005 **

Interpretation:
  → Less physical/emotional hurt → Higher confidence in responses
  → EVAhurt is the only significant predictor for confidence
  → Other mood dimensions don't affect response confidence

================================================================================
KEY INSIGHTS
================================================================================

1. EVAmood is the most consistent predictor
   - Significant for 4 out of 5 thought dimensions
   - Strongest effects on valence (β=3.571) and selfother (β=3.473)

2. Different mood facets predict different thought aspects
   - EVAmood → valence, selfother, onoff, time
   - EVAfeel → onoff, time, selfother (often with opposite sign to EVAmood)
   - EVAtense → selfother only
   - EVAhurt → confidence only

3. Multivariate approach reveals suppression effects
   - When all EVA dimensions are included together, some effects emerge
     that might be masked in univariate analyses
   - EVAfeel often has opposite effects to EVAmood, suggesting they capture
     distinct aspects of mood state

4. Group differences
   - Only selfother shows significant group effect
   - Risk of Depression group has higher self-focus (β=12.07, p=0.020)
   - This suggests mood effects on thought content are similar across groups,
     but baseline self-focus differs

5. Clinical implications
   - Mood state significantly influences thought content and quality
   - Interventions targeting mood could potentially modulate thought patterns
   - Self-focus in depression may be trait-like rather than state-dependent

================================================================================
STATISTICAL NOTES
================================================================================

- All EVA predictors were centered before fitting (mean = 0)
- Mixed models account for repeated measures within subjects
- Group was coded with Controls as reference category
- Some models show singular random effects covariance (warnings)
  This suggests limited between-subject variance, but fixed effects are valid
- AIC/BIC values are NaN due to convergence method used (LBFGS)

================================================================================
COMPARISON WITH UNIVARIATE MODELS
================================================================================

The previous univariate analysis (each EVA dimension separately) showed:
  - 17 significant predictors across all combinations
  - total_score was significant for valence, selfother, onoff, confidence

The multivariate analysis (all EVA dimensions together) shows:
  - More nuanced pattern with 10 significant effects
  - Reveals suppression effects (e.g., EVAfeel negative for onoff/time)
  - Better identifies which specific mood facet drives each effect
  - Controls for collinearity between EVA dimensions

Recommendation: Use multivariate results for interpretation as they control
for shared variance between mood dimensions.
